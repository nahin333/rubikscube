\title{COVE-3: Potential-Free Coboundary and Clique-Syndrome Compilation\\
for Budget-Preserving Interactive Cube Search}
\author{Rubik's Graph Lab}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}

Heuristic search frequently generates successors that are immediately
pruned by distance bounds or starve one ply later under canonical move
restrictions. We study these overheads in COVE-3, a compact two-phase
Rubik's Cube solver, across two cumulative compilation stages that
preserve exact scalar move sequences and logical node budgets. First,
1-step contour compilation encodes paired phase-2 distances and operator
masks into 16-bit dictionary indices (\(40,609\) signatures across
\(1,935,360\) entries), cutting median solve latency from \(6.19\) ms to
\(2.20\) ms on \(500\) states while avoiding \(79.33\%\) of recursive
calls; two follow-up variants---exact-cost failure replay and
deferred-charge chain traversal---preserve the count contract but fail
to improve latency, showing that random table reads and two-step dead
ends dominate runtime. Second, we diagnose why 1-step contours stall:
\(53.81\%\) of surviving phase-2 calls are dead-on-arrival Horizon-2
ghosts, while phase-1 branching (\(b_1=18\)) overflows 32-bit words and
16-bit dictionaries (\(>620,000\) signatures). We overcome both barriers
via \textbf{potential-free dual-horizon compilation}, storing zero
scalar distance bits in either phase. In phase 1, we prove that each
outer face's coset forms a \(K_4\) clique in every abstract Cayley
graph, collapsing three-move contours into 4-bit clique syndromes (24
bits per state) decoded across all 18 moves by constant-time SWAR and
integrated via a linear coboundary law. In phase 2, omitting scalar
potentials quotients level sets and frees 10 bits for unary canonical
Horizon-2 survival masks inside a single unified dictionary of
\(24,743\) signatures---\(39.07\%\) fewer entries and \(62,440\) fewer
persistent bytes than 1-step COVE-3---recovering root distances by
steepest-descent 1-form walks. A three-tier batched accounting rule
charges Horizon-1, Horizon-2, and unary-starved Horizon-3 subtrees in
\(O(1)\) via a 1-KiB lookup table, and a communication-complexity
reduction proves Horizon 2 maximal for factored unary accounting. On a
fresh held-out 500-state corpus, potential-free dual-horizon compilation
cuts generated visits by a further \(68.47\%\) beyond COVE-3
(\(93.62\%\) versus scalar), lowers median latency on \(499/500\) states
(\(2.57\) ms \(\to\) \(1.84\) ms in three-way evaluation; \(1.43\) ms
pairwise), and outperforms even a 32-bit distance-storing variant while
saving \(2.00\) MiB.

\end{abstract}

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}}

A browser solver must balance response latency, memory, solution
quality, and deterministic correctness. Exact graph exploration quickly
becomes expensive on the 3×3 Rubik's Cube, whose fixed-frame state space
contains 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 legal positions. Practical two-phase
search offers an effective interactive alternative, yet scalar
implementations spend most of their time constructing coordinates and
stack frames that existing heuristic bounds immediately reject.

Our starting point is the project's scalar two-phase solver, frozen at
its
\href{https://github.com/nahin333/rubikscube/commit/e7023a8d96681c6a4c23a19c4c4ecb5208b910e4}{pre-change
revision}. In addition to global node and time limits, the solver caps
phase-2 visits at 250,000 logical visits per phase-1 subgroup endpoint.
Simply deleting pruned recursive calls silently enlarges that allowance
and changes which solution is returned---even under default solver
settings. Preserving exact scalar count-based behavior provides an
unusually clean experimental control: every candidate solver must return
the exact same move sequence, phase split, and logical visit count on
every state, isolating execution efficiency from solution-length
tradeoffs.

In our first optimization stage (COVE-3 contour compilation), we
compiled paired phase-2 distance tables into dictionaries of 24-bit
\((d, M_0, M_-)\) signatures, reducing recursive visits by \(79.33\%\)
(\(129.25\)M \(\to\) \(26.72\)M) and median latency from \(6.19\) ms to
\(2.20\) ms. However, two subsequent algorithmic
experiments---exact-cost failure replay and deferred-charge
single-successor chain traversal---exposed a sharp wall: neither reduced
latency despite saving millions of logical visits or recursive calls.
Profiling the remaining \(26.72\)M visits revealed two fundamental
structural bottlenecks:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Uncompiled Phase-1 Signature Explosion (\(b_1 = 18\)):} Naive
  \((d, M_0, M_-)\) signatures require \(4 + 18 + 18 = 40\) bits
  (exceeding 32-bit JS bitwise registers) and produce \(623,078\)
  (\texttt{coSl}) and \(633,263\) (\texttt{eoSl}) distinct signatures,
  overflowing 16-bit dictionary indices (\(65,536\)) by nearly an order
  of magnitude. Phase 1 therefore remained scalar (\(9.08\)M visits,
  \(92.48\%\) immediately pruned).
\item
  \textbf{Phase-2 Horizon-1 Ghost Admissibility (\(53.81\%\) dead on
  arrival):} Out of \(17.64\)M generated phase-2 visits in COVE-3,
  \(9.49\)M (\(53.81\%\)) pass Horizon-1 filtering yet immediately find
  zero admissible children
  (\(A_{\text{face}(j)} \cap F_C(c', r-1) \cap F_E(e', r-1) = \emptyset\)):
  \(49.60\%\) die from unary canonical-move exclusions (for example,
  canonical opposite-face ordering forbids 6 of 10 phase-2 moves after
  face \texttt{D}), and \(50.40\%\) die from cross-abstraction mask
  disjointness.
\end{enumerate}

In this work, we resolve both bottlenecks simultaneously through
\textbf{potential-free coboundary and Cayley-clique dual-horizon
compilation}, eliminating stored scalar distances from both phases
altogether.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{contributions}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item

\item
  \textbf{Cayley-\(K_4\) clique-syndrome SWAR compilation for Phase 1.}
  Exploiting the \(K_4\) coset clique structure of outer-face turns in
  the half-turn Cayley graph, we prove that
  \(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0} \implies M_0^{(f)}(a) = \mathbf{1}\),
  reducing per-face contour patterns from \(2^6 = 64\) to \(15\)

  into 24 potential-free bits (3 bytes/state), decode in parallel via
  branchless SWAR, and integrate child distances via the closed-form
  coboundary identity
  \(D(\delta(a, 3f+q)) - D(a) = 1 - \mu_f(a) - v_{f,q}(a)\).
\item
  \textbf{Potential-free unified coboundary Horizon-2 dictionary for
  Phase 2.} Observing that \((M_-, M_0)\) is the exact discrete
  coboundary \(dD\) of \(D\), we omit scalar distances
  completely---recovering root potentials once per endpoint via
  steepest-descent 1-form walks and freeing 10 bits per 32-bit
  dictionary entry to store unary canonical Horizon-2 survival masks
  \(M_-^{(2)}\) (Proposition 5). Unifying both phase-2 abstractions
  yields \(24,743\) total signatures, shrinking the phase-2 memory

  encoding two-step lookahead.
\item
  \textbf{Three-tier ghost accounting and communication-complexity
  barrier.} We derive an exact three-tier batched accounting rule
  (Algorithm 2, Proposition 6) covering Horizon-1 rejections, Horizon-2
  ghosts, and unary-starved Horizon-3 subtrees via a 1-KiB lookup table
  and short-circuited child hoisting, and prove via two-party
  set-disjointness / inner-product communication complexity that Horizon
  2 is the unique maximal lookahead depth for factored unary exact
  accounting (Proposition 7).
\item
  \textbf{Exhaustive verification and held-out cache-aware evaluation.}
  Across all \(57,087,450\) abstract transitions, \(1,935,360\) 1-form
  walks, \(439\) fixtures, \(139\) budget boundaries, a constructive
  multi-tier witness, and a fresh held-out 500-state evaluation

  \(68.47\%\) beyond COVE-3 (\(93.62\%\) vs.~scalar) and improving

\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{proposed-technical-novelty}{%
\subsection{Proposed technical novelty}\label{proposed-technical-novelty}}

Two-phase solving \cite{kociemba_coordinates}, pattern databases
\cite{korf1997}, selective successor generation \cite{felner2012},
\cite{goldenberg2014}, relative/modular PDB compression
\cite{kociemba_pruning}, \cite{sturtevant2017}, \cite{breyer2010}, and
transposition caching \cite{akagi2010} are established prior art. Within
reviewed literature, our proposed technical novelty lies in the
synthesis of: (i) potential-free coboundary quotienting with
steepest-descent 1-form root recovery to embed canonical Horizon-2
operator masks in fewer dictionary entries than 1-step OSFs; (ii) 4-bit
Cayley-\(K_4\) clique syndromes with parallel SWAR decoding and linear
coboundary integration; (iii) multi-tier batched scalar-budget
accounting across Horizons 1--3; and (iv) the communication-complexity
barrier characterizing the exact lookahead limit of factored unary
tables. Worldwide priority across all unpublished solvers or non-indexed
venues is not claimed; scope and overlap are documented in the
\href{https://github.com/nahin333/rubikscube/blob/main/research/NOVELTY.md}{dated
novelty review}.

\hypertarget{related-work}{%
\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}}

\hypertarget{pattern-databases-and-two-phase-solving}{%
\subsection{Pattern databases and two-phase solving}\label{pattern-databases-and-two-phase-solving}}

Korf combines IDA* with pattern databases (PDBs) to find optimal Rubik's
Cube solutions \cite{korf1997}. Kociemba's two-phase algorithm reaches
the subgroup \(H=\langle U,D,R^2,L^2,F^2,B^2\rangle\) before solving
within \(H\) \cite{kociemba_coordinates}, and documents modulo-3
distance compression \cite{kociemba_pruning}. Breyer and Korf's 1.6-bit
PDBs store \(h \bmod 3\) potentials and reconstruct absolute heuristic
values by walking toward \(h=0\) \cite{breyer2010}. Unlike mod-3
tables---which store compressed scalar potentials and must scan
neighbors at runtime to infer \(\Delta h\)---our potential-free
compilation stores \textbf{zero scalar potential bits} and instead
stores explicit coboundary operator masks \((M_-, M_0, M_-^{(2)})\) and
\(K_4\) syndromes directly for \(O(1)\) bit-parallel move filtering,
using steepest descent along \(M_-\) only once at phase roots.

The external software comparator is cubejs 1.3.2 \cite{cubejs},
previously used by the web application. Because cubejs targets
\(\le 22\)-move solutions whereas COVE-3 accepts its first verified
completion within fixed budgets, we treat cubejs as an application-level
reference and use our pinned frozen scalar solver (\texttt{e7023a8d})
and pinned 1-step contour solver (\texttt{f26aa421}) for matched-output
attribution.

\hypertarget{selective-successor-generation-and-multi-step-lookahead}{%
\subsection{Selective successor generation and multi-step lookahead}\label{selective-successor-generation-and-multi-step-lookahead}}

Felner et al.~\cite{felner2012} and Goldenberg et
al.~\cite{goldenberg2014} introduce Enhanced Partial Expansion A*
(EPEA*) and EPE-IDA*, using domain or pattern-database
operator-selection functions (OSFs) so search generates only children
matching a target \(\Delta f\). Goldenberg et al.~\cite{goldenberg2014}
observe that extending OSFs beyond one-step lookahead typically causes
severe table blowup because operator combinations grow exponentially
with horizon depth. Our coboundary dictionary circumvents this blowup in
Phase 2: discarding the 4-bit scalar potential \(D(a)\) merges
equivalent contour neighborhoods across distance levels, freeing 10 bits
in a 32-bit word for canonical Horizon-2 survival masks while
\emph{reducing} total dictionary cardinality from \(40,609\) (1-step
with \(D\)) to \(24,743\) (2-step potential-free, unified across both
abstractions).

\hypertarget{value-compression-caching-and-traversal-refinement}{%
\subsection{Value compression, caching, and traversal refinement}\label{value-compression-caching-and-traversal-refinement}}

Sturtevant, Felner, and Helmert study heuristic value compression
\cite{sturtevant2017}, whereas our compiler losslessly shares complete
coboundary and multi-horizon operator masks. McAleer et al.~investigate
learned cube heuristics \cite{mcaleer2018}, Fridrich documents human
speedcubing \cite{fridrich}, Demaine et al.~analyze asymptotic
\(N\times N\times N\) bounds \cite{demaine2011}, and Rokicki et
al.~establish the 20-turn half-turn diameter \cite{rokicki_gods_number};
none addresses exact count-budget compilation of fixed-3×3 browser
search. Akagi et al.~analyze IDA* transposition-table correctness
\cite{akagi2010}, and Lammich and Neumann formalize DFS refinement
\cite{lammich2015}, grounding our follow-up negative-result variants in
Sections 4.6--4.7.

\hypertarget{problem-formulation}{%
\section{Problem Formulation}\label{problem-formulation}}

Let \(s_0\) be a legal 3×3 state, \(s_\star\) the solved state, and
\(T\) the move transition function in the half-turn metric (each
outer-face turn \(F, F^2, F'\) has unit cost). Legal cubie states
\((p_c, p_e, o_c, o_e)\) satisfy

\[
\begin{gathered}
\sum_{i=0}^{7}o_{c,i}\equiv0\pmod3,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{11}o_{e,i}\equiv0\pmod2,\\
\operatorname{parity}(p_c)=\operatorname{parity}(p_e).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

Phase 1 searches all \(b_1=18\) moves to reach \(H\) (\(o_c=0, o_e=0\),
middle-slice edges in slice), guided by
\(h_1(c_1,e_1,s_1)=\max\{D_{CO}(c_1,s_1), D_{EO}(e_1,s_1)\}\) over
coordinates of sizes \(2187\), \(2048\), and \(495\). Phase 2 searches
the \(b_2=10\) moves preserving \(H\) over corner permutation \(c\),
U/D-edge permutation \(e\), and middle-slice permutation \(q\) (sizes
\(40320\), \(40320\), and \(24\)), guided by

\[
h_2(c,e,q)=\max\{D_C(c,q),D_E(e,q)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consecutive same-face moves are forbidden, and opposite commuting faces
enforce a canonical order (\texttt{allowed(face,\ last)}), yielding
canonical allowed move masks \(A_\ell^{(1)}\) (\(18\), \(15\), or \(12\)
moves in Phase 1) and \(A_\ell^{(2)}\) (\(10\), \(9\), \(8\), or \(7\)
moves in Phase 2). A \textbf{logical visit} counts every node visited by
the frozen scalar DFS (including immediately pruned nodes); a
\textbf{generated visit} counts nodes for which the compiled solver
actually enters a recursive DFS frame or leaf check.

every \(65,536\) logical visits.

\hypertarget{stage-1-exact-contour-filters}{%
\subsection{Stage-1 exact contour filters}\label{stage-1-exact-contour-filters}}

For any undirected unit-cost abstract graph with exact goal distance
\(D(a)\) and transition \(\delta(a,m)\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_-(a)&=\{m:D(\delta(a,m))=D(a)-1\},\\
M_0(a)&=\{m:D(\delta(a,m))\le D(a)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

At remaining depth \(r\ge D(a)\) with slack \(\sigma = r - D(a)\), the
moves whose child satisfies \(D(\delta(a,m)) \le r - 1\) are

\[
F(a,r)=
\begin{cases}
M_-(a),&\sigma=0,\\
M_0(a),&\sigma=1,\\
\mathcal A,&\sigma\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Proposition 1 --- Exact Horizon-1 successor filtering.} For an
admitted node at remaining depth \(r\),
\(m \in A_\ell \cap F_1(a_1,r) \cap F_2(a_2,r)\) if and only if \(m\) is
canonical after \(\ell\) and
\(\max\{D_1(\delta_1(a_1,m)), D_2(\delta_2(a_2,m))\} \le r - 1\).

\emph{Proof.} Every generator has a unit-cost inverse in the same phase,
so \(|D(\delta(a,m)) - D(a)| \le 1\). Case analysis on
\(\sigma \in \{0, 1, \ge 2\}\) yields Eq. (4), and bitmask intersection
implements the maximum in Eq. (2) together with canonical filtering
\(A_\ell\). ∎

\hypertarget{stage-1-dictionary-representation}{%
\subsection{Stage-1 dictionary representation}\label{stage-1-dictionary-representation}}

In Stage 1
(\texttt{expansion:\ \textquotesingle{}contour\textquotesingle{}}), each
phase-2 state forms the 24-bit word
\(\sigma(a)=(D(a)\ll 20)\mathbin{|}(M_0(a)\ll 10)\mathbin{|}M_-(a)\),
interned via 16-bit \texttt{Uint16} IDs into separate corner (\(5,806\)
entries) and edge (\(34,803\) entries) \texttt{Uint32} dictionaries
(\(U=40,609\) total). With transition tables \(M_T=1,783,560\) B,
phase-1 byte distances \(D_1=2,096,325\) B, and \(D_2=1,935,360\)
phase-2 entries, scalar storage is \(M_{\rm scalar}=5,815,245\) B
(\(5.55\) MiB) and Stage-1 contour storage is
\(M_{\rm contour}=M_T+D_1+2D_2+4U=7,913,041\) B (\(7.55\) MiB).

\hypertarget{preserving-the-search-budget-at-horizon-1}{%
\subsection{Preserving the search budget at Horizon 1}\label{preserving-the-search-budget-at-horizon-1}}

\begin{figure}[t]
\hrule\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Algorithm 1.} Budget-preserving Horizon-1 phase-2 expansion
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\footnotesize,breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true]
Input: phase-2 coordinates (c,e,q), depth r, last face l
charge one generated visit; enforce guards
if h2(c,e,q) > r: return failure
if r = 0: return whether coordinates are solved
A = canonical move mask after l
M = A & cornerContour(c,q,r) & edgeContour(e,q,r)
for each set bit m of M, in original move order:
  charge uncharged rejected moves of A preceding m
  stop immediately if a budget is exhausted
  construct child coordinates and push m
  if recursive search succeeds: return success
  pop m; stop if a budget is exhausted
charge remaining rejected moves of A
return failure
\end{Verbatim}
\vspace{3pt}\hrule
\end{figure}

\textbf{Proposition 2 --- Horizon-1 count-budget trace equivalence.}
Under identical tables, move order, and node caps, scalar search and
Algorithm 1 return the identical move sequence, split, and logical visit
count, or terminate at the identical count guard, whenever no wall-clock
timeout intervenes.

\emph{Proof.} By Proposition 1, every move in \(A_\ell \setminus M\)
enters scalar recursion, increments the visit and endpoint counters by
\(1\), fails \(h_2 > r - 1\), and returns immediately. Batch-charging
those contiguous single-visit rejections in sibling order while clamping
at \(B+1\), \(G+1\), and every \(2048\)-visit checkpoint preserves all
observable counter states by induction on DFS depth. ∎

\hypertarget{exact-cost-failure-certificates}{%
\subsection{Exact-cost failure certificates}\label{exact-cost-failure-certificates}}

An exhausted phase-2 subtree \(x=(c,e,q,r,\ell)\) that finishes without
hitting \(B\) has a deterministic logical visit count \(w(x)\). After
charging the root visit, a cached entry \((x,w)\) may safely replace
traversal if and only if

\[
w(x)-1\le\min\{B-n_E,\ G-n_G\}.
\tag{5}
\]

\textbf{Proposition 3 --- Failure-certificate replay.} Replacing an
exhausted subtree satisfying Eq. (5) by charging \(w(x)-1\) logical
visits across all \(2048\)-visit checkpoints preserves returned moves,
count cutoffs, and progress reports when wall-clock timeouts do not
intervene.

\emph{Proof.} In Phase 2, \((c,e,q)\) uniquely determines the group
element in \(H\), and \((r,\ell)\) uniquely determines the ordered
finite search tree. Full-key matching (\texttt{Float64} exact integer
\(<2^{53}\)) prevents collision aliasing, and Eq. (5) guarantees neither
count guard triggers mid-subtree, so induction over replayed subtrees
preserves exact counter states. ∎

\hypertarget{deferred-sibling-charges-along-single-successor-chains}{%
\subsection{Deferred sibling charges along single-successor chains}\label{deferred-sibling-charges-along-single-successor-chains}}

When \(|M|=1\), rejected siblings preceding the unique survivor are
charged immediately while rejected siblings following it accumulate as
scalar debt along an iterative descent chain; debt is discarded upon
success and batch-charged upon failure, preserving Proposition 2's
contract without recursive frames.

\hypertarget{cayley-clique-syndrome-swar-and-closed-form-coboundary-law}{%
\subsection{Cayley-clique syndrome SWAR and closed-form coboundary law}\label{cayley-clique-syndrome-swar-and-closed-form-coboundary-law}}

Why could Stage 1 not compile Phase 1? Encoding \((D, M_0, M_-)\)
naively for \(b_1=18\) moves requires \(4+18+18=40\) bits and yields
\(623,078\) (\texttt{coSl}) and \(633,263\) (\texttt{eoSl}) unique
signatures, overflowing both 32-bit bitwise words and 16-bit dictionary
IDs. We overcome both obstacles algebraically.

Partition the 18 generators into the 6 outer faces
\(f \in \{0,\dots,5\}\), where face \(f\) contributes the three
non-identity coset turns \(\{F, F^2, F'\}\) indexed by
\(q \in \{0,1,2\}\). Let \(M_-^{(f)}(a), M_0^{(f)}(a) \in \{0,1\}^3\) be
the restriction of Eq. (3) to face \(f\).

\textbf{Proposition 4 --- Cayley-\(K_4\) clique implication, 4-bit
syndrome SWAR, and linear coboundary law.} Let \(\mathcal G\) be any
quotient graph of the 3×3 cube group under the half-turn metric with
exact goal distance \(D\). For every state \(a\) and outer face \(f\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(\(K_4\) clique implication)} If
  \(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}\), then
  \(M_0^{(f)}(a) = \mathbf{1} = (1,1,1)\). Consequently, out of
  \(2^6=64\) binary pairs \((M_-^{(f)}, M_0^{(f)})\), at most
  \(7 + 8 = 15\) are realizable.
\item
  \emph{(4-bit syndrome \& branchless SWAR)} Define the 1-bit descent
  indicator \(\mu_f(a) = [M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}]\) and 3-bit
  payload \(v_f(a) = \mu_f(a) ? M_-^{(f)}(a) : M_0^{(f)}(a)\), packed at
  nibble \(4f\) of a 24-bit word
  \(W(a) = \sum_{f=0}^{5} ((\mu_f(a)\ll 3)\mathbin{|}v_f(a)) \ll 4f\).
  Then across all 6 faces simultaneously, using 32-bit arithmetic with
  \(\text{mask3} = ((W(a)\ggg 3)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x111111})\times 7\):
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  M_-(a) &= W(a)\mathbin{\&}\text{mask3},\\
  M_0(a) &= (W(a)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x777777})\mathbin{|}\text{mask3},
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{6}
  \] where move \(3f+q\) occupies bit position \(p=4f+q\)
  (\(f = p\ggg 2\), \(3f+q = p - f\)).
\item
  \emph{(Closed-form clique coboundary identity)} Every transition
  \(m = 3f + q\) satisfies the exact integer recurrence \[
  D(\delta(a, 3f+q)) - D(a) = 1 - \mu_f(a) - v_{f,q}(a).
  \tag{7}
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) For any two distinct elements
\(g_1, g_2 \in \{I, F, F^2, F^3\}\), \(g_1^{-1}g_2 \in \{F, F^2, F^3\}\)
is a single half-turn-metric generator. Thus the four coset projections
\(\{a, F(a), F^2(a), F^3(a)\}\) form a complete subgraph \(K_4\) (with
self-loops if projections collide) in \(\mathcal G\), so
\(\max_g D(g(a)) - \min_g D(g(a)) \le 1\). If
\(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}\), some \(g_\star \in \{F,F^2,F^3\}\) has
\(D(g_\star(a)) = D(a)-1\). Any \(g \in \{F,F^2,F^3\}\) is adjacent to
\(g_\star(a)\), so \(D(g(a)) \le D(g_\star(a)) + 1 = D(a)\), giving
\(M_0^{(f)}(a) = (1,1,1)\). Thus \(\mu_f=1\) admits \(2^3-1=7\) non-zero
patterns \(v_f \in \{1,\dots,7\}\) and \(\mu_f=0\) admits \(2^3=8\)
patterns \(v_f \in \{0,\dots,7\}\), totaling \(15\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\arabic{enumi})}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\item
  Extracting \(\mu_f\) via \((W\ggg 3)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x111111}\)
  isolates bit \(0\) of each nibble without cross-nibble carry;
  multiplying by \(7 = (111)_2\) replicates \(\mu_f\) across bits
  \(\{0,1,2\}\) of nibble \(f\) because \(7 < 16\) prevents inter-nibble
  overflow. When \(\mu_f=1\), nibble \(f\) of \(\text{mask3}\) is \(7\),
  giving \(W\mathbin{\&}7 = v_f = M_-^{(f)}\) and
  \((v_f)\mathbin{|}7 = 7 = M_0^{(f)}\) by (1). When \(\mu_f=0\), nibble
  \(f\) of \(\text{mask3}\) is \(0\), giving
  \(W\mathbin{\&}0 = 0 = M_-^{(f)}\) and
  \((W\mathbin{\&}7)\mathbin{|}0 = v_f = M_0^{(f)}\). Since
  \(p = 4f + q\) with \(q \in \{0,1,2\}\), \(\lfloor p/4 \rfloor = f\)
  and \(p - f = 3f + q\).
\item
  If \(\mu_f(a)=1\), part (1) implies
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) \in \{D(a)-1, D(a)\}\) with
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) = D(a)-1 \iff v_{f,q}(a)=1\), so
  \(\Delta D = -v_{f,q}(a) = 1 - 1 - v_{f,q}(a)\). If \(\mu_f(a)=0\),
  \(M_-^{(f)}(a)=\mathbf{0}\), so
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) \in \{D(a), D(a)+1\}\) with
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) = D(a) \iff v_{f,q}(a)=1\), giving
  \(\Delta D = 1 - v_{f,q}(a) = 1 - 0 - v_{f,q}(a)\). ∎
\end{enumerate}

Exhaustive compilation across all \(2,096,325\) Phase-1 entries
(\texttt{coSl} and \texttt{eoSl}) confirms that all \(15\) syndromes
occur and stores each state in 3 potential-free bytes (\texttt{co24},
\texttt{eo24}), eliminating raw \texttt{coSl}/\texttt{eoSl} distance
arrays completely.

\hypertarget{potential-free-unified-coboundary-horizon-2-dictionary}{%
\subsection{Potential-free unified coboundary Horizon-2 dictionary}\label{potential-free-unified-coboundary-horizon-2-dictionary}}

In Phase 2, \(53.81\%\) of COVE-3's generated visits pass Horizon-1
filtering only to starve immediately at the child. Half of those ghosts
occur even within a single abstraction when \(\sigma = 0\)
(\(r = D(a) \ge 2\)): move \(j \in M_-(a)\) reaches \(a' = \delta(a,j)\)
at \(D(a') = r - 1 \ge 1\), where survival requires
\(A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)} \cap M_-(a') \ne \emptyset\). Define the
\textbf{unary canonical Horizon-2 survival mask}:

\[
M_-^{(2)}(a) = \bigl\{j \in M_-(a) : A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)} \cap M_-(\delta(a,j)) \ne \emptyset\bigr\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Storing \((D(a), M_-^{(2)}(a), M_0(a), M_-(a))\) naively requires
\(4 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 34 > 32\) bits and fractures dictionary sharing
across distance shells. Instead, we observe that \((M_-(a), M_0(a))\) is
already the exact discrete 1-form (coboundary \(dD\)) of \(D\),
rendering \(D(a)\) completely redundant.

\textbf{Proposition 5 --- Potential-free coboundary recovery and unified
Horizon-2 dictionary.} Pack the 30-bit potential-free word

\[
\omega(a) = \bigl(M_-^{(2)}(a)\ll 20\bigr)\mathbin{|}\bigl(M_0(a)\ll 10\bigr)\mathbin{|}M_-(a).
\tag{9}
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(Root recovery)} On any connected finite unit-cost graph with
  unique goal \(a_\star\) (\(D(a_\star)=0\)), starting from any \(a_0\)
  and repeatedly stepping to \(\delta(a, \min M_-(a))\) while
  \(a \ne a_\star\) terminates in \textbf{exact} \(D(a_0)\) steps.
\item
  \emph{(Path integration)} Along any edge \(j\),
  \(D(\delta(a,j)) = D(a) - [j \in M_-(a)] + [j \notin M_0(a)]\).
\item
  \emph{(Level-set quotienting)} Interning \(\omega(a)\) jointly across
  all \(1,935,360\) corner-slice and edge-slice states yields
  \(U_{\rm H2} = 24,743 < 2^{16}\) shared signatures (\(98,972\)
  bytes)---\(39.07\%\) fewer entries and \(63,464\) fewer dictionary
  bytes than Stage-1 COVE-3 (\(40,609\) entries, \(162,436\) bytes).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) For every \(a \ne a_\star\), \(D(a) \ge 1\) on a
connected graph, so a shortest path to \(a_\star\) provides at least one
neighbor with \(D(\delta(a,j)) = D(a)-1\); hence
\(M_-(a) \ne \emptyset\). Every step decreases \(D\) by \(1\), reaching
the unique \(D=0\) state \(a_\star\) in \(D(a_0)\) steps. (2) Since
\(\Delta D \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\), \(\Delta D = -1 \iff j \in M_-(a)\) and
\(\Delta D = +1 \iff j \notin M_0(a)\), matching the formula in all
three cases. (3) Isomorphic local coboundary neighborhoods at distinct
distances \(d_1 \ne d_2\) collide under \(\omega(a)\), verified
exhaustively across all \(1,935,360\) entries. ∎

\hypertarget{multi-tier-ghost-accounting-and-the-horizon-2-barrier}{%
\subsection{Multi-tier ghost accounting and the Horizon-2 barrier}\label{multi-tier-ghost-accounting-and-the-horizon-2-barrier}}

Pruning beyond Horizon 1 introduces a fundamental accounting subtlety: a
move pruned at Horizon 2 or Horizon 3 would \textbf{not} have cost \(1\)
visit in scalar DFS. Specifically, let \(x=(c,e,q)\) be an admitted
phase-2 node at remaining depth \(r \ge 2\) after face \(\ell\), with
\(H_1(x) = A_\ell^{(2)} \cap F_C(c,q,r) \cap F_E(e,q,r)\) and unary
Horizon-2 filtered mask

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x) = H_1(x) &\cap (\sigma_C = 0 \ ? \ M_-^{(2)}(c,q) : \mathcal A_2)\\
&\cap (\sigma_E = 0 \ ? \ M_-^{(2)}(e,q) : \mathcal A_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Precompute a 1,024-byte lookup table
\(\text{extra}[M] = \sum_{j \in M} |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\) for all
10-bit masks \(M \subseteq \{0,\dots,9\}\).

\begin{figure}[t]
\hrule\vspace{5pt}

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\footnotesize,breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true]

\textbf{Proposition 6 --- Three-tier count-budget trace equivalence.}
Under identical inputs and node caps, Algorithm 2 (together with hoisted
Phase-1 SWAR ghost charging) visits a strict subset of Stage-1 generated
nodes while returning the exact scalar move sequence, phase split,
logical visit count, endpoint cutoffs, and periodic progress checkpoints
whenever wall-clock timeouts do not intervene.

\emph{Proof.} Every non-recursed canonical move \(j \in A_\ell^{(2)}\)
falls into one of three mutually exclusive tiers at child
\(y = \delta(x,j)\) (remaining depth \(r' = r - 1 \ge 1\)):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tier 1 (\(j \notin H_1(x)\)):} \(h_2(y) > r'\), so scalar DFS
  visits \(y\) (\(1\) visit) and returns immediately.
\item
  \textbf{Tier 2 (\(j \in H_1(x)\) and \(H_1(y) = \emptyset\)):} Either
  \(j \in \text{dead1} = H_1(x)\setminus C(x)\) (certified without child
  lookup by Eq. (8)) or hoisted lookup yields \(\text{mc1}=0\) or
  \(\text{nh1}=0\). Because \(j \in H_1(x)\), \(h_2(y) \le r'\) passes
  at \(y\), \(r' \ge 1\) enters \(y\)'s loop, and every
  \(k \in A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}\) fails \(h_2(\delta(y,k)) > r'-1\)
  in \(1\) visit. Thus \(y\)'s exact scalar subtree cost is
  \(1 + |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\), matching both
  \texttt{popcount(pre)\ +\ extra{[}pre\ \&\ dead1{]}} (which
  contributes \(1 + |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\) for
  \(j \in \text{pre}\cap\text{dead1}\) and \(1\) for
  \(j \in \text{pre}\setminus\text{dead1}\)) and
  \texttt{1\ +\ \textbar{}A\_\{face(j)\}\textbar{}}.
\item
  \textbf{Tier 3 (\(j \in C(x)\), \(H_1(y) = \text{nh1} \ne \emptyset\),
  \(r' \ge 2\), and \(\text{ncand} = \emptyset\)):} Here \(y\) passes
  \(h_2(y) \le r'\); each
  \(k \in A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)} \setminus \text{nh1}\) fails at depth
  \(r'-1\) in \(1\) visit, whereas each \(k \in \text{nh1}\) passes at
  child \(z = \delta(y,k)\) (depth \(r'-1 \ge 1\)) but is certified by
  unary \(M_-^{(2)}\) of \(y\) to have \(H_1(z) = \emptyset\), incurring
  \(1 + |A_{\text{face}(k)}^{(2)}|\) visits. Summing over \(y\) and all
  \(k \in A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}\) gives \[
  \begin{aligned}
  1 + \bigl(|A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}| - |\text{nh1}|\bigr) &+ \sum_{k \in \text{nh1}} \bigl(1 + |A_{\text{face}(k)}^{(2)}|\bigr)\\
  &= 1 + |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}| + \text{extra}[\text{nh1}].
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{11}
  \]
\end{itemize}

In all three tiers, skipped scalar visits form a contiguous failed
prefix before the next surviving branch, so \texttt{skip} clamping at
\(B+1\), \(G+1\), and multiples of \(2048\) reproduces every scalar
guard and checkpoint identically. Phase-1 SWAR hoisting applies Tier 1
(\(1\) visit) and Tier 2 (\(1 + |A_{\text{face}(m)}^{(1)}|\) visits)
analogously without an endpoint cap. ∎

Notice why Tier 3 requires hoisting \(y\)'s word (\texttt{nh1}) rather
than pruning lookup-free at parent \(x\):

\textbf{Proposition 7 --- Horizon-2 communication-complexity barrier for
factored unary tables.} Consider two factored abstractions
\(\mathcal A_1, \mathcal A_2\) whose states \((x_1, x_2)\) are
summarized independently by unary parent table entries
\(\tau_1(x_1), \tau_2(x_2)\) without constructing coupled child states
\((y_1, y_2)\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Horizon 2 is achievable} for unary starvation
  (\(A_f \cap F_i(\delta_i(x_i,j), r-1) = \emptyset\)), with exact
  scalar cost \(1 + |A_f|\).
\item
  \textbf{Coupled Horizon-2 exhaustion}
  (\(A_f \cap F_1(y_1,r-1) \cap F_2(y_2,r-1) = \emptyset\) when each
  unary intersection is non-empty) and \textbf{exact Horizon-3 scalar
  visit accounting} from \((\tau_1(x_1), \tau_2(x_2))\) alone require
  \(\Omega(|A_f|)\) bits of cross-abstraction information per move
  \(j\), preventing small shared unary dictionaries from certifying
  general Horizon-3 counts without hoisting child lookups.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} Fix a move \(j\) with allowed child move set \(A_f\) of
size \(m = |A_f|\), and associate arbitrary subsets
\(U = A_f \cap F_1(y_1, r-1) \subseteq \{0,1\}^m\) and
\(V = A_f \cap F_2(y_2, r-1) \subseteq \{0,1\}^m\) to the independent
abstractions. Deciding whether \(y\) is a coupled Horizon-2 ghost is
equivalent to two-party Set Disjointness
\(\operatorname{DISJ}_m(U, V) = [U \cap V = \emptyset]\), and
determining the Horizon-3 scalar visit count
\(1 + m + \sum_{k \in U \cap V} |A_{\text{face}(k)}|\) determines
weighted inner product \(\sum_{k} w_k U_k V_k\) (\(w_k \ge 7\)). Any
factored representation \(\tau_1(x_1), \tau_2(x_2)\) that determines
\(\operatorname{DISJ}_m(U,V)\) across realized \((U,V)\) pairs must
distinguish all \(2^m\) subsets on each side (requiring \(m\) bits per
move \(j\), or \(10 \times 8 = 80\) bits across all moves of \(x\),
overflowing 16-bit dictionaries). Hoisting \((y_1, y_2)\) only on unary
Horizon-2 survivors resolves \(\text{nh1} = U \cap V\) explicitly in
\(O(1)\) bitwise operations while short-circuiting \(y_2\) whenever
\(U = \emptyset\). ∎

default unconstrained output equivalence.

\hypertarget{limitations}{%
\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}}

\textbf{Novelty and worst-case bounds.} Two-phase search, PDBs,
selective generation, relative PDB compression, and bit-parallel masks
have rich prior literature. While our potential-free coboundary
Horizon-2 dictionary, Cayley-\(K_4\) SWAR syndromes, three-tier budget
accounting, and Horizon-2 barrier proof were not found in reviewed
sources, we do not claim certified worldwide priority or any improvement
in exponential worst-case time complexity.

\textbf{Storage and compilation tradeoffs.} Although \texttt{h2only}
(\(7.49\) MiB) is smaller than Stage-1 COVE-3 (\(7.55\) MiB), full
dual-horizon compilation (\texttt{dual}, \(11.49\) MiB) increases
persistent typed-array memory by \(51.8\%\) over Stage-1 COVE-3
(\(107.1\%\) over raw scalar bytes) and adds \(478.8\) ms of worker
compilation after the first scalar solve. Peak V8 heap and physical
mobile-device cache behavior remain unmeasured.

\textbf{Search policy and comparator scope.} Count-budget equivalence
holds whenever wall-clock deadlines do not intervene; faster execution
can still alter real-time timeout outcomes. External \texttt{cubejs}
timings reflect different move-length targets, and native C++/Rust or
symmetry-reduced \texttt{min2phase} solvers are outside our
pure-JavaScript browser scope. Only 3×3 two-phase solving is compiled
(with 2×2/3×3 exact search retained for pedagogical modes, and manual
sticker verification required for camera inputs).

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

Diagnosing why 1-step contour compilation stalled---Phase-1 signature
explosion (\(b_1=18\)) and \(53.81\%\) Phase-2 Horizon-1 ghost
expansions---led us to \textbf{potential-free dual-horizon compilation}:
representing heuristic tables purely by their discrete coboundaries with
zero stored scalar distances. In Phase 1, the Cayley-\(K_4\) clique
structure of outer-face turns compresses 18-move contours into 24-bit
SWAR syndromes with a closed-form linear coboundary update; in Phase 2,
level-set quotienting fits unary canonical Horizon-2 masks into a
unified \(24,743\)-entry dictionary smaller than 1-step COVE-3, paired
with an \(O(1)\) three-tier ghost-accounting rule proved tight up to a
Horizon-2 communication-complexity barrier. Across 500 held-out states,
COVE-3 preserves exact scalar solutions and node budgets while cutting
generated visits by \(93.62\%\) relative to scalar (\(68.47\%\) beyond
Stage-1 COVE-3) and reducing median solve latency down to
\(1.43\)--\(1.84\) ms.
