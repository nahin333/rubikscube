\begin{abstract}

Practical Rubik's Cube search faces two coupled bottlenecks: scalar
two-phase DFS wastes over \(90\%\) of its work on immediately pruned or
two-step-dead branches, while greedy first-completion Phase-1 search
plunges into high-frustration subgroup-\(H\) endpoints averaging
\(\approx 23\) moves. We address both problems across three cumulative
stages. First, in \textbf{COVE-3}, 1-step contour dictionaries
(\(40,609\) Phase-2 signatures) cut median latency from \(6.19\) ms to
\(2.20\) ms while preserving exact scalar move sequences and logical
node budgets; two count-preserving extensions (failure replay and chain
traversal) yield negative latency results that isolate random table
reads and Horizon-2 dead ends (\(53.81\%\) of surviving Phase-2 visits)
as the true bottlenecks. Second, \textbf{potential-free dual-horizon
compilation} eliminates stored scalar distances from both phases: 4-bit
Cayley-\(K_4\) clique syndromes (\(24\) bits/state) decode all 18
Phase-1 moves via branchless SWAR, while level-set quotienting fits
unary canonical Horizon-2 survival masks into a unified \(24,743\)-entry
Phase-2 dictionary (\(62,440\) B smaller than 1-step COVE-3) paired with
\(O(1)\) three-tier ghost accounting proved maximal for factored unary
tables. On 500 held-out states (\texttt{seed\ 0xd48a63}), dual-horizon
compilation cuts generated visits by \(93.62\%\) (\(1.84\) ms 3-way;
\(1.43\) ms pairwise) at 100\% identical outputs. Third, moving beyond
fixed-trajectory compilation, we introduce \textbf{Holonomy-Guided
Bifurcated Eikonal Wavefront Search (HBEW)} (\(+2.54\) MiB), a novel
solving algorithm viewing \(\pi: G \to G/H\) as a discrete principal
\(H\)-bundle with exact path-length defect
\(L(p,q)=h_1(s)+\sigma_1(p)+h_2(s\cdot p)+\sigma_2(s\cdot p,q)\). After
showing that pure subgroup metric-distortion shortcuts
(\(\mathcal W_5 \subset H\)) fail generically (\(0/60\) improved), we
prove four synergistic mechanisms: an equatorial UD-slice funnel
(\(4/495\) distance-1 and \(55/495\) distance-2 cosets; Proposition 8),
a \(K_2\) quarter-turn sister-fiber involution co-integrating paired
lifts \(y_- = y_+ F^2\) (\(7,741,440\) verified transitions; Proposition
9), 4-bit phase-crossing Bellman potentials with inline zero-slack
sieving (Proposition 10), and \(\sigma_2\)-stratified Eikonal wavefront
verification (Theorem 1). On a fresh 500-state held-out corpus
(\texttt{seed\ 0x5e71a9}), HBEW lowers mean length from \(22.94\) to
\(20.58\) moves (\(20.52\) exact-shell; \(500/500 \le 22\),
\(47.0\% \le 20\)) in \(4.84\) ms median while pruning \(74.4\%\) of
harvested subgroup lifts; on 200 shared scrambles
(\texttt{seed\ 0x74bc39}), HBEW strictly Pareto-dominates
\texttt{cubejs\ 1.3.2} (\(6.54\times\) faster median, \(15.7\times\)
faster P95, and \(1.25\) fewer moves: \(20.46\) vs.~\(21.71\)).

\end{abstract}

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}}

A browser solver must balance response latency, memory, solution
quality, and deterministic correctness. Exact graph exploration quickly
becomes expensive on the 3×3 Rubik's Cube, whose fixed-frame state space
contains 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 legal positions. Practical two-phase
search offers an effective interactive alternative, yet scalar
implementations spend most of their time constructing coordinates and
stack frames that existing heuristic bounds immediately reject.

Our starting point is the project's scalar two-phase solver, frozen at
its
\href{https://github.com/nahin333/rubikscube/commit/e7023a8d96681c6a4c23a19c4c4ecb5208b910e4}{pre-change
revision}. In addition to global node and time limits, the solver caps
phase-2 visits at 250,000 logical visits per phase-1 subgroup endpoint.
Simply deleting pruned recursive calls silently enlarges that allowance
and changes which solution is returned---even under default solver
settings. Preserving exact scalar count-based behavior provides an
unusually clean experimental control: every candidate solver must return
the exact same move sequence, phase split, and logical visit count on
every state, isolating execution efficiency from solution-length
tradeoffs.

In our first optimization stage (COVE-3 contour compilation), we
compiled paired phase-2 distance tables into dictionaries of 24-bit
\((d, M_0, M_-)\) signatures, reducing recursive visits by \(79.33\%\)
(\(129.25\)M \(\to\) \(26.72\)M) and median latency from \(6.19\) ms to
\(2.20\) ms. However, two subsequent algorithmic
experiments---exact-cost failure replay and deferred-charge
single-successor chain traversal---exposed a sharp wall: neither reduced
latency despite saving millions of logical visits or recursive calls.
Profiling the remaining \(26.72\)M visits revealed two fundamental
structural bottlenecks:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Uncompiled Phase-1 Signature Explosion (\(b_1 = 18\)):} Naive
  \((d, M_0, M_-)\) signatures require \(4 + 18 + 18 = 40\) bits
  (exceeding 32-bit JS bitwise registers) and produce \(623,078\)
  (\texttt{coSl}) and \(633,263\) (\texttt{eoSl}) distinct signatures,
  overflowing 16-bit dictionary indices (\(65,536\)) by nearly an order
  of magnitude. Phase 1 therefore remained scalar (\(9.08\)M visits,
  \(92.48\%\) immediately pruned).
\item
  \textbf{Phase-2 Horizon-1 Ghost Admissibility (\(53.81\%\) dead on
  arrival):} Out of \(17.64\)M generated phase-2 visits in COVE-3,
  \(9.49\)M (\(53.81\%\)) pass Horizon-1 filtering yet immediately find
  zero admissible children
  (\(A_{\text{face}(j)} \cap F_C(c', r-1) \cap F_E(e', r-1) = \emptyset\)):
  \(49.60\%\) die from unary canonical-move exclusions (for example,
  canonical opposite-face ordering forbids 6 of 10 phase-2 moves after
  face \texttt{D}), and \(50.40\%\) die from cross-abstraction mask
  disjointness.
\end{enumerate}

In this work, we first resolve both compilation bottlenecks through
\textbf{potential-free coboundary and Cayley-clique dual-horizon
compilation} (Stages 1--2), and then address the fundamental
solution-quality ceiling of greedy two-phase search itself
(\texttt{22.94} mean moves, \texttt{max\ 25–26}) by proposing a new
solving algorithm: \textbf{Holonomy-Guided Bifurcated Eikonal Wavefront
Search (HBEW, Stage 3)}. Viewing \(\pi: G \to G/H\) as a discrete
principal \(H\)-bundle reveals that Phase 1 walks on \(G/H\) blind to
vertical subgroup frustration \(h_2(y) \in H\), while
continued-shrinking IDA* (\texttt{cubejs} \cite{cubejs}) wastes tens to
hundreds of milliseconds searching deep Phase-2 trees on
high-frustration fibers before encountering low-holonomy fibers later in
the shell.

\begin{enumerate}

\tightlist

\item
  \textbf{Cayley-\(K_4\) clique-syndrome SWAR compilation for Phase 1.}
  Exploiting the \(K_4\) coset clique structure of outer-face turns in

  \(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0} \implies M_0^{(f)}(a) = \mathbf{1}\),
  reducing per-face contour patterns from \(2^6 = 64\) to \(15\)

  into 24 potential-free bits (3 bytes/state), decode in parallel via
  branchless SWAR, and integrate child distances via the closed-form

\item
  \textbf{Potential-free unified coboundary Horizon-2 dictionary for

  steepest-descent 1-form walks and freeing 10 bits per 32-bit

  \(M_-^{(2)}\) (Proposition 5). Unifying both phase-2 abstractions
  yields \(24,743\) total signatures, shrinking the phase-2 memory

  \textbf{Three-tier ghost accounting and communication-complexity

  (Algorithm 2, Proposition 6) covering Horizon-1 rejections, Horizon-2

  and short-circuited child hoisting, and prove via two-party
  set-disjointness / inner-product communication complexity that Horizon
  2 is the unique maximal lookahead depth for factored unary exact
  accounting (Proposition 7).
\item
  \textbf{Exhaustive Stage-2 verification and held-out cache-aware
  evaluation.} Across all \(57,087,450\) abstract transitions,
  \(1,935,360\) 1-form walks, \(439\) fixtures, \(139\) budget
  boundaries, a constructive multi-tier witness, and a fresh held-out

  vs.~scalar) and improving latency on \(499/500\) states.
\item
  \textbf{Stage-3 Holonomy-Guided Bifurcated Eikonal Wavefront Search
  (HBEW).} Formulating two-phase search on the principal \(H\)-bundle
  \(G \to G/H\), we document a negative result on pure subgroup
  metric-distortion ``wormholes'' (\(\mathcal W_5 \subset H\)) and prove
  four coupled mechanisms (Algorithm 3): the \(\binom{12}{4}\)
  equatorial UD-slice funnel (\(4/495\) dist-1, \(55/495\) dist-2;

  (Proposition 10), and \(\sigma_2\)-stratified Eikonal wavefront
  verification with provable bounded-shell optimality (Theorem 1). On a

  achieves \(20.58\) mean moves (\(100\% \le 22\)) in \(4.84\) ms median

  faster median, \(15.7\times\) faster P95, \(20.46\) vs.~\(21.71\)

\end{enumerate}

layers: (a) on the \textbf{compiler layer (COVE-3)}, potential-free
coboundary quotienting with 1-form steepest-descent root recovery for
sub-1-step-sized Horizon-2 dictionaries, 24-bit Cayley-\(K_4\) clique
syndromes, three-tier scalar budget accounting, and the Horizon-2
communication-complexity barrier; and (b) on the
\textbf{solver-algorithm layer (HBEW)}, coupling equatorial UD-slice

phase-crossing Bellman holonomy potentials \(\Psi_r\), inline zero-slack
Horizon-2 sieving, and \(\sigma_2\)-stratified Eikonal wavefront
verification over a single coordinate axis without inverse-cube or
multi-axis probing. Worldwide priority across all unpublished solvers or
non-indexed venues is not claimed; scope and overlap are documented in
the

\hypertarget{related-work}{%
\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}}

\hypertarget{pattern-databases-and-two-phase-solving}{%
\subsection{Pattern databases and two-phase solving}\label{pattern-databases-and-two-phase-solving}}

Korf combines IDA* with pattern databases (PDBs) to find optimal Rubik's
Cube solutions \cite{korf1997}. Kociemba's two-phase algorithm reaches
the subgroup \(H=\langle U,D,R^2,L^2,F^2,B^2\rangle\) before solving
within \(H\) \cite{kociemba_coordinates}, and documents modulo-3
distance compression \cite{kociemba_pruning}. Breyer and Korf's 1.6-bit
PDBs store \(h \bmod 3\) potentials and reconstruct absolute heuristic
values by walking toward \(h=0\) \cite{breyer2010}. Unlike mod-3
tables---which store compressed scalar potentials and must scan
neighbors at runtime to infer \(\Delta h\)---our potential-free
compilation stores \textbf{zero scalar potential bits} and instead
stores explicit coboundary operator masks \((M_-, M_0, M_-^{(2)})\) and
\(K_4\) syndromes directly for \(O(1)\) bit-parallel move filtering,
using steepest descent along \(M_-\) only once at phase roots.

The external software comparator is cubejs 1.3.2 \cite{cubejs},
previously used by the web application. Because cubejs targets
\(\le 22\)-move solutions whereas COVE-3 accepts its first verified
completion within fixed budgets, we treat cubejs as an application-level
reference and use our pinned frozen scalar solver (\texttt{e7023a8d})
and pinned 1-step contour solver (\texttt{f26aa421}) for matched-output
attribution.

\hypertarget{selective-successor-generation-and-multi-step-lookahead}{%
\subsection{Selective successor generation and multi-step lookahead}\label{selective-successor-generation-and-multi-step-lookahead}}

Felner et al.~\cite{felner2012} and Goldenberg et
al.~\cite{goldenberg2014} introduce Enhanced Partial Expansion A*
(EPEA*) and EPE-IDA*, using domain or pattern-database
operator-selection functions (OSFs) so search generates only children
matching a target \(\Delta f\). Goldenberg et al.~\cite{goldenberg2014}
observe that extending OSFs beyond one-step lookahead typically causes
severe table blowup because operator combinations grow exponentially
with horizon depth. Our coboundary dictionary circumvents this blowup in
Phase 2: discarding the 4-bit scalar potential \(D(a)\) merges
equivalent contour neighborhoods across distance levels, freeing 10 bits
in a 32-bit word for canonical Horizon-2 survival masks while
\emph{reducing} total dictionary cardinality from \(40,609\) (1-step
with \(D\)) to \(24,743\) (2-step potential-free, unified across both
abstractions).

\hypertarget{value-compression-caching-and-traversal-refinement}{%
\subsection{Value compression, caching, and traversal refinement}\label{value-compression-caching-and-traversal-refinement}}

Sturtevant, Felner, and Helmert study heuristic value compression
\cite{sturtevant2017}, whereas our compiler losslessly shares complete
coboundary and multi-horizon operator masks. McAleer et al.~investigate
learned cube heuristics \cite{mcaleer2018}, Fridrich documents human
speedcubing \cite{fridrich}, Demaine et al.~analyze asymptotic
\(N\times N\times N\) bounds \cite{demaine2011}, and Rokicki et
al.~establish the 20-turn half-turn diameter \cite{rokicki_gods_number};
none addresses exact count-budget compilation of fixed-3×3 browser
search. Akagi et al.~analyze IDA* transposition-table correctness
\cite{akagi2010}, and Lammich and Neumann formalize DFS refinement
\cite{lammich2015}, grounding our follow-up negative-result variants in
Sections 4.6--4.7.

\hypertarget{multi-solution-two-phase-search-and-eikonal-wavefronts}{%
\subsection{Multi-solution two-phase search and Eikonal wavefronts}\label{multi-solution-two-phase-search-and-eikonal-wavefronts}}

Standard multi-solution Kociemba solvers (\texttt{cubejs} \cite{cubejs})
shorten solutions by running chronological Phase-1 IDA* across
increasing depths \(d_1\) and invoking full Phase-2 IDA* up to
\(B - d_1 - 1\) at every \(H\)-entry, shrinking \(B\) upon each
completion. Because Phase 1 has zero visibility into vertical subgroup
distance \(h_2(y)\), early high-frustration fibers trigger deep
\(\sigma_2 > 0\) Phase-2 searches before zero-slack fibers later in the
same shell are even reached. Engineering-oriented solvers
(\texttt{min2phase}) mitigate tail spikes externally via 3-axis
\(\times\) inverse-cube probing, pre-moves, and \(D_{4h}\) symmetry
reductions. By contrast, HBEW operates on a \textbf{single coordinate
axis without inverse probing, pre-moves, or symmetry tables}: it
forecasts \(h_2\) above \(\partial H\) via Bellman potentials, harvests
\(K_2\)-paired sister fibers, and propagates a discrete minimum-arrival
wavefront across \((b_0, \sigma_2)\) strata---an algebraic counterpart
on coset bundles to monotone Eikonal fast-marching wavefronts on
continuous domains \cite{sethian1996}.

\hypertarget{problem-formulation}{%
\section{Problem Formulation}\label{problem-formulation}}

Let \(s_0\) be a legal 3×3 state, \(s_\star\) the solved state, and
\(T\) the move transition function in the half-turn metric (each
outer-face turn \(F, F^2, F'\) has unit cost). Legal cubie states
\((p_c, p_e, o_c, o_e)\) satisfy

\[
\begin{gathered}
\sum_{i=0}^{7}o_{c,i}\equiv0\pmod3,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{11}o_{e,i}\equiv0\pmod2,\\
\operatorname{parity}(p_c)=\operatorname{parity}(p_e).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

Phase 1 searches all \(b_1=18\) moves to reach \(H\) (\(o_c=0, o_e=0\),
middle-slice edges in slice), guided by
\(h_1(c_1,e_1,s_1)=\max\{D_{CO}(c_1,s_1), D_{EO}(e_1,s_1)\}\) over
coordinates of sizes \(2187\), \(2048\), and \(495\). Phase 2 searches
the \(b_2=10\) moves preserving \(H\) over corner permutation \(c\),
U/D-edge permutation \(e\), and middle-slice permutation \(q\) (sizes
\(40320\), \(40320\), and \(24\)), guided by

\[
h_2(c,e,q)=\max\{D_C(c,q),D_E(e,q)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consecutive same-face moves are forbidden, and opposite commuting faces
enforce a canonical order (\texttt{allowed(face,\ last)}), yielding
canonical allowed move masks \(A_\ell^{(1)}\) (\(18\), \(15\), or \(12\)
moves in Phase 1) and \(A_\ell^{(2)}\) (\(10\), \(9\), \(8\), or \(7\)
moves in Phase 2). A \textbf{logical visit} counts every node visited by
the frozen scalar DFS (including immediately pruned nodes); a
\textbf{generated visit} counts nodes for which the compiled solver
actually enters a recursive DFS frame or leaf check.

every \(65,536\) logical visits.

\hypertarget{stage-1-exact-contour-filters}{%
\subsection{Stage-1 exact contour filters}\label{stage-1-exact-contour-filters}}

For any undirected unit-cost abstract graph with exact goal distance
\(D(a)\) and transition \(\delta(a,m)\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_-(a)&=\{m:D(\delta(a,m))=D(a)-1\},\\
M_0(a)&=\{m:D(\delta(a,m))\le D(a)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

At remaining depth \(r\ge D(a)\) with slack \(\sigma = r - D(a)\), the
moves whose child satisfies \(D(\delta(a,m)) \le r - 1\) are

\[
F(a,r)=
\begin{cases}
M_-(a),&\sigma=0,\\
M_0(a),&\sigma=1,\\
\mathcal A,&\sigma\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Proposition 1 --- Exact Horizon-1 successor filtering.} For an
admitted node at remaining depth \(r\),
\(m \in A_\ell \cap F_1(a_1,r) \cap F_2(a_2,r)\) if and only if \(m\) is
canonical after \(\ell\) and
\(\max\{D_1(\delta_1(a_1,m)), D_2(\delta_2(a_2,m))\} \le r - 1\).

\emph{Proof.} Every generator has a unit-cost inverse in the same phase,
so \(|D(\delta(a,m)) - D(a)| \le 1\). Case analysis on
\(\sigma \in \{0, 1, \ge 2\}\) yields Eq. (4), and bitmask intersection
implements the maximum in Eq. (2) together with canonical filtering
\(A_\ell\). ∎

\hypertarget{stage-1-dictionary-representation}{%
\subsection{Stage-1 dictionary representation}\label{stage-1-dictionary-representation}}

In Stage 1
(\texttt{expansion:\ \textquotesingle{}contour\textquotesingle{}}), each
phase-2 state forms the 24-bit word
\(\sigma(a)=(D(a)\ll 20)\mathbin{|}(M_0(a)\ll 10)\mathbin{|}M_-(a)\),
interned via 16-bit \texttt{Uint16} IDs into separate corner (\(5,806\)
entries) and edge (\(34,803\) entries) \texttt{Uint32} dictionaries
(\(U=40,609\) total). With transition tables \(M_T=1,783,560\) B,
phase-1 byte distances \(D_1=2,096,325\) B, and \(D_2=1,935,360\)
phase-2 entries, scalar storage is \(M_{\rm scalar}=5,815,245\) B
(\(5.55\) MiB) and Stage-1 contour storage is
\(M_{\rm contour}=M_T+D_1+2D_2+4U=7,913,041\) B (\(7.55\) MiB).

\hypertarget{preserving-the-search-budget-at-horizon-1}{%
\subsection{Preserving the search budget at Horizon 1}\label{preserving-the-search-budget-at-horizon-1}}

\begin{figure}[t]
\hrule\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Algorithm 1.} Budget-preserving Horizon-1 phase-2 expansion
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\footnotesize,breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true]
Input: phase-2 coordinates (c,e,q), depth r, last face l
charge one generated visit; enforce guards
if h2(c,e,q) > r: return failure
if r = 0: return whether coordinates are solved
A = canonical move mask after l
M = A & cornerContour(c,q,r) & edgeContour(e,q,r)
for each set bit m of M, in original move order:
  charge uncharged rejected moves of A preceding m
  stop immediately if a budget is exhausted
  construct child coordinates and push m
  if recursive search succeeds: return success
  pop m; stop if a budget is exhausted
charge remaining rejected moves of A
return failure
\end{Verbatim}
\vspace{3pt}\hrule
\end{figure}

\textbf{Proposition 2 --- Horizon-1 count-budget trace equivalence.}
Under identical tables, move order, and node caps, scalar search and
Algorithm 1 return the identical move sequence, split, and logical visit
count, or terminate at the identical count guard, whenever no wall-clock
timeout intervenes.

\emph{Proof.} By Proposition 1, every move in \(A_\ell \setminus M\)
enters scalar recursion, increments the visit and endpoint counters by
\(1\), fails \(h_2 > r - 1\), and returns immediately. Batch-charging
those contiguous single-visit rejections in sibling order while clamping
at \(B+1\), \(G+1\), and every \(2048\)-visit checkpoint preserves all
observable counter states by induction on DFS depth. ∎

\hypertarget{complexity-comparison}{%
\subsection{Complexity comparison}\label{complexity-comparison}}

Let \(b_1\le 18\), \(b_2=10\), \(d_1\le 12\), \(d_2\le 18\), and let
\(k_v\) (Horizon-1 survivors) and \(k_v^{(2)} \le k_v\) (Horizon-2

\hypertarget{cayley-clique-syndrome-swar-and-closed-form-coboundary-law}{%
\subsection{Cayley-clique syndrome SWAR and closed-form coboundary law}\label{cayley-clique-syndrome-swar-and-closed-form-coboundary-law}}

Why could Stage 1 not compile Phase 1? Encoding \((D, M_0, M_-)\)
naively for \(b_1=18\) moves requires \(4+18+18=40\) bits and yields
\(623,078\) (\texttt{coSl}) and \(633,263\) (\texttt{eoSl}) unique
signatures, overflowing both 32-bit bitwise words and 16-bit dictionary
IDs. We overcome both obstacles algebraically.

Partition the 18 generators into the 6 outer faces
\(f \in \{0,\dots,5\}\), where face \(f\) contributes the three
non-identity coset turns \(\{F, F^2, F'\}\) indexed by
\(q \in \{0,1,2\}\). Let \(M_-^{(f)}(a), M_0^{(f)}(a) \in \{0,1\}^3\) be
the restriction of Eq. (3) to face \(f\).

\textbf{Proposition 4 --- Cayley-\(K_4\) clique implication, 4-bit
syndrome SWAR, and linear coboundary law.} Let \(\mathcal G\) be any
quotient graph of the 3×3 cube group under the half-turn metric with
exact goal distance \(D\). For every state \(a\) and outer face \(f\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(\(K_4\) clique implication)} If
  \(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}\), then
  \(M_0^{(f)}(a) = \mathbf{1} = (1,1,1)\). Consequently, out of
  \(2^6=64\) binary pairs \((M_-^{(f)}, M_0^{(f)})\), at most
  \(7 + 8 = 15\) are realizable.
\item
  \emph{(4-bit syndrome \& branchless SWAR)} Define the 1-bit descent
  indicator \(\mu_f(a) = [M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}]\) and 3-bit
  payload \(v_f(a) = \mu_f(a) ? M_-^{(f)}(a) : M_0^{(f)}(a)\), packed at
  nibble \(4f\) of a 24-bit word
  \(W(a) = \sum_{f=0}^{5} ((\mu_f(a)\ll 3)\mathbin{|}v_f(a)) \ll 4f\).
  Then across all 6 faces simultaneously, using 32-bit arithmetic with
  \(\text{mask3} = ((W(a)\ggg 3)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x111111})\times 7\):
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  M_-(a) &= W(a)\mathbin{\&}\text{mask3},\\
  M_0(a) &= (W(a)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x777777})\mathbin{|}\text{mask3},
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{6}
  \] where move \(3f+q\) occupies bit position \(p=4f+q\)
  (\(f = p\ggg 2\), \(3f+q = p - f\)).
\item
  \emph{(Closed-form clique coboundary identity)} Every transition
  \(m = 3f + q\) satisfies the exact integer recurrence \[
  D(\delta(a, 3f+q)) - D(a) = 1 - \mu_f(a) - v_{f,q}(a).
  \tag{7}
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) For any two distinct elements
\(g_1, g_2 \in \{I, F, F^2, F^3\}\), \(g_1^{-1}g_2 \in \{F, F^2, F^3\}\)
is a single half-turn-metric generator. Thus the four coset projections
\(\{a, F(a), F^2(a), F^3(a)\}\) form a complete subgraph \(K_4\) (with
self-loops if projections collide) in \(\mathcal G\), so
\(\max_g D(g(a)) - \min_g D(g(a)) \le 1\). If
\(M_-^{(f)}(a) \ne \mathbf{0}\), some \(g_\star \in \{F,F^2,F^3\}\) has
\(D(g_\star(a)) = D(a)-1\). Any \(g \in \{F,F^2,F^3\}\) is adjacent to
\(g_\star(a)\), so \(D(g(a)) \le D(g_\star(a)) + 1 = D(a)\), giving
\(M_0^{(f)}(a) = (1,1,1)\). Thus \(\mu_f=1\) admits \(2^3-1=7\) non-zero
patterns \(v_f \in \{1,\dots,7\}\) and \(\mu_f=0\) admits \(2^3=8\)
patterns \(v_f \in \{0,\dots,7\}\), totaling \(15\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\arabic{enumi})}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\item
  Extracting \(\mu_f\) via \((W\ggg 3)\mathbin{\&}\text{0x111111}\)
  isolates bit \(0\) of each nibble without cross-nibble carry;
  multiplying by \(7 = (111)_2\) replicates \(\mu_f\) across bits
  \(\{0,1,2\}\) of nibble \(f\) because \(7 < 16\) prevents inter-nibble
  overflow. When \(\mu_f=1\), nibble \(f\) of \(\text{mask3}\) is \(7\),
  giving \(W\mathbin{\&}7 = v_f = M_-^{(f)}\) and
  \((v_f)\mathbin{|}7 = 7 = M_0^{(f)}\) by (1). When \(\mu_f=0\), nibble
  \(f\) of \(\text{mask3}\) is \(0\), giving
  \(W\mathbin{\&}0 = 0 = M_-^{(f)}\) and
  \((W\mathbin{\&}7)\mathbin{|}0 = v_f = M_0^{(f)}\). Since
  \(p = 4f + q\) with \(q \in \{0,1,2\}\), \(\lfloor p/4 \rfloor = f\)
  and \(p - f = 3f + q\).
\item
  If \(\mu_f(a)=1\), part (1) implies
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) \in \{D(a)-1, D(a)\}\) with
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) = D(a)-1 \iff v_{f,q}(a)=1\), so
  \(\Delta D = -v_{f,q}(a) = 1 - 1 - v_{f,q}(a)\). If \(\mu_f(a)=0\),
  \(M_-^{(f)}(a)=\mathbf{0}\), so
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) \in \{D(a), D(a)+1\}\) with
  \(D(\delta(a,3f+q)) = D(a) \iff v_{f,q}(a)=1\), giving
  \(\Delta D = 1 - v_{f,q}(a) = 1 - 0 - v_{f,q}(a)\). ∎
\end{enumerate}

Exhaustive compilation across all \(2,096,325\) Phase-1 entries
(\texttt{coSl} and \texttt{eoSl}) confirms that all \(15\) syndromes
occur and stores each state in 3 potential-free bytes (\texttt{co24},
\texttt{eo24}), eliminating raw \texttt{coSl}/\texttt{eoSl} distance
arrays completely.

\hypertarget{potential-free-unified-coboundary-horizon-2-dictionary}{%
\subsection{Potential-free unified coboundary Horizon-2 dictionary}\label{potential-free-unified-coboundary-horizon-2-dictionary}}

In Phase 2, \(53.81\%\) of COVE-3's generated visits pass Horizon-1
filtering only to starve immediately at the child. Half of those ghosts
occur even within a single abstraction when \(\sigma = 0\)
(\(r = D(a) \ge 2\)): move \(j \in M_-(a)\) reaches \(a' = \delta(a,j)\)
at \(D(a') = r - 1 \ge 1\), where survival requires
\(A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)} \cap M_-(a') \ne \emptyset\). Define the
\textbf{unary canonical Horizon-2 survival mask}:

\[
M_-^{(2)}(a) = \bigl\{j \in M_-(a) : A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)} \cap M_-(\delta(a,j)) \ne \emptyset\bigr\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Storing \((D(a), M_-^{(2)}(a), M_0(a), M_-(a))\) naively requires
\(4 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 34 > 32\) bits and fractures dictionary sharing
across distance shells. Instead, we observe that \((M_-(a), M_0(a))\) is
already the exact discrete 1-form (coboundary \(dD\)) of \(D\),
rendering \(D(a)\) completely redundant.

\textbf{Proposition 5 --- Potential-free coboundary recovery and unified
Horizon-2 dictionary.} Pack the 30-bit potential-free word

\[
\omega(a) = \bigl(M_-^{(2)}(a)\ll 20\bigr)\mathbin{|}\bigl(M_0(a)\ll 10\bigr)\mathbin{|}M_-(a).
\tag{9}
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(Root recovery)} On any connected finite unit-cost graph with
  unique goal \(a_\star\) (\(D(a_\star)=0\)), starting from any \(a_0\)
  and repeatedly stepping to \(\delta(a, \min M_-(a))\) while
  \(a \ne a_\star\) terminates in \textbf{exact} \(D(a_0)\) steps.
\item
  \emph{(Path integration)} Along any edge \(j\),
  \(D(\delta(a,j)) = D(a) - [j \in M_-(a)] + [j \notin M_0(a)]\).
\item
  \emph{(Level-set quotienting)} Interning \(\omega(a)\) jointly across
  all \(1,935,360\) corner-slice and edge-slice states yields
  \(U_{\rm H2} = 24,743 < 2^{16}\) shared signatures (\(98,972\)
  bytes)---\(39.07\%\) fewer entries and \(63,464\) fewer dictionary
  bytes than Stage-1 COVE-3 (\(40,609\) entries, \(162,436\) bytes).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) For every \(a \ne a_\star\), \(D(a) \ge 1\) on a
connected graph, so a shortest path to \(a_\star\) provides at least one
neighbor with \(D(\delta(a,j)) = D(a)-1\); hence
\(M_-(a) \ne \emptyset\). Every step decreases \(D\) by \(1\), reaching
the unique \(D=0\) state \(a_\star\) in \(D(a_0)\) steps. (2) Since
\(\Delta D \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\), \(\Delta D = -1 \iff j \in M_-(a)\) and
\(\Delta D = +1 \iff j \notin M_0(a)\), matching the formula in all
three cases. (3) Isomorphic local coboundary neighborhoods at distinct
distances \(d_1 \ne d_2\) collide under \(\omega(a)\), verified
exhaustively across all \(1,935,360\) entries. ∎

Pruning beyond Horizon 1 introduces a fundamental accounting subtlety: a
move pruned at Horizon 2 or Horizon 3 would \textbf{not} have cost \(1\)
visit in scalar DFS. Specifically, let \(x=(c,e,q)\) be an admitted
phase-2 node at remaining depth \(r \ge 2\) after face \(\ell\), with

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x) = H_1(x) &\cap (\sigma_C = 0 \ ? \ M_-^{(2)}(c,q) : \mathcal A_2)\\

\tag{10}
\]

Precompute a 1,024-byte lookup table
\(\text{extra}[M] = \sum_{j \in M} |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\) for all
10-bit masks \(M \subseteq \{0,\dots,9\}\).

\textbf{Proposition 6 --- Three-tier count-budget trace equivalence.}
Under identical inputs and node caps, Algorithm 2 (together with hoisted
Phase-1 SWAR ghost charging) visits a strict subset of Stage-1 generated
nodes while returning the exact scalar move sequence, phase split,
logical visit count, endpoint cutoffs, and periodic progress checkpoints
whenever wall-clock timeouts do not intervene.

\emph{Proof.} Every non-recursed canonical move \(j \in A_\ell^{(2)}\)
falls into one of three mutually exclusive tiers at child
\(y = \delta(x,j)\) (remaining depth \(r' = r - 1 \ge 1\)):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tier 1 (\(j \notin H_1(x)\)):} \(h_2(y) > r'\), so scalar DFS
  visits \(y\) (\(1\) visit) and returns immediately.
\item
  \textbf{Tier 2 (\(j \in H_1(x)\) and \(H_1(y) = \emptyset\)):} Either
  \(j \in \text{dead1} = H_1(x)\setminus C(x)\) (certified without child
  lookup by Eq. (8)) or hoisted lookup yields \(\text{mc1}=0\) or
  \(\text{nh1}=0\). Because \(j \in H_1(x)\), \(h_2(y) \le r'\) passes
  at \(y\), \(r' \ge 1\) enters \(y\)'s loop, and every
  \(k \in A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}\) fails \(h_2(\delta(y,k)) > r'-1\)
  in \(1\) visit. Thus \(y\)'s exact scalar subtree cost is
  \(1 + |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\), matching both
  \texttt{popcount(pre)\ +\ extra{[}pre\ \&\ dead1{]}} (which
  contributes \(1 + |A_{\text{face}(j)}^{(2)}|\) for
  \(j \in \text{pre}\cap\text{dead1}\) and \(1\) for
  \(j \in \text{pre}\setminus\text{dead1}\)) and
  \texttt{1\ +\ \textbar{}A\_\{face(j)\}\textbar{}}.
\item

  \(h_2(y) \le r'\); each

\hypertarget{principal-h-bundle-defect-decomposition-and-ud-slice-funnel}{%
\subsection{Principal H-bundle defect decomposition and UD-slice funnel}\label{principal-h-bundle-defect-decomposition-and-ud-slice-funnel}}

Stages 1 and 2 accelerate COVE-3 down to \(\approx 1.4\)--\(1.8\) ms
while deliberately preserving its greedy first-completion trajectory
(\texttt{22.94} mean moves). Before redesigning the search algorithm
itself, we tested whether subgroup paths \(q \in H\) could be shortened
directly via \textbf{metric-distortion ``Cayley wormholes''}---short
ambient words \(w \in G\) returning to \(H\) whose \(G\)-cost beats
their \(H\)-distance (\(|w|_G < d_H(e, w)\)). Exhaustive enumeration up
to ambient length \(5\) uncovered \(128\) words
\(\mathcal W_5 \subset H\) of \(G\)-length \(5\) and \(H\)-distance
\(7\) (\(\Delta h_2 = -2\), such as \(F R^2 F'\)-conjugated slice
commutators). Yet across \(60\) uniform Phase-1 endpoints,
\(\mathcal W_5\) macro-steps improved \textbf{\(0 / 60\)} solutions:
their branching factor \(128^{1/5} \approx 2.64\) is far below canonical
Phase-2 branching (\(\approx 6.59\)), making post-hoc shortcutting
exponentially unlikely to align with arbitrary \((c,e,q) \in H\).

Instead, view the quotient projection \(\pi: G \to G/H\)
(\(|G/H| = 2,217,093,120\)) as a discrete \textbf{principal
\(H\)-bundle} with structure group \(H\) (\(|H| = 19,508,428,800\)). Any
two-phase trajectory \((p, q)\) from \(s \in G\)
(\(s \cdot p = y \in H\), \(y \cdot q = e\)) has exact length

\[
L(p, q) = \underbrace{h_1(s) + \sigma_1(p)}_{d_1 = |p|} + \underbrace{h_2(y) + \sigma_2(y, q)}_{d_2 = |q|},
\tag{12}
\]

where \(\sigma_1(p) = |p| - h_1(s) \ge 0\) is the \textbf{horizontal
base slack}, \(h_2(y)\) is the \textbf{vertical subgroup holonomy} at
fiber \(y = s \cdot p \in H\), and
\(\sigma_2(y, q) = |q| - h_2(y) \ge 0\) is the \textbf{vertical subgroup
slack}. Minimizing \(L(p,q)\) across bounded shells \(d_1 \le D_1\) is
equivalent to minimizing total bundle defect
\(\Delta(p,q) = \sigma_1(p) + h_2(s \cdot p) + \sigma_2(s \cdot p, q)\).

Near \(\partial H\), the \(\binom{12}{4} = 495\) UD-slice coordinate
imposes a severe combinatorial bottleneck:

\textbf{Proposition 8 --- Equatorial UD-slice funnel into \(H\).} Let
\(\mathcal S = \binom{\{0,\dots,11\}}{4}\) (\(|\mathcal S| = 495\)) be
the UD-slice coset graph with solved equatorial slice
\(s_\star = \{8,9,10,11\}\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Every Phase-1 trajectory entering \(H\) from \(G \setminus H\)
  (\texttt{d1\ \textgreater{}=\ 1}) terminates with an
  \textbf{equatorial quarter-turn} \(F^{\pm 1}\) for some
  \(F \in \mathcal E = \{R, F, L, B\}\). Neither \(U, D\) turns nor
  half-turns \(F^2\) can transition \(G \setminus H \to H\).
\item
  Exactly \textbf{\(4\) out of \(495\)} slice states stand at distance
  \(1\) from \(s_\star\), and at most \textbf{\(55\) out of \(495\)}
  stand at distance \(2\). Precomputing a 495-byte 8-bit mask
  \texttt{slDist2Mask} rejects \(92.19\%\) of state-move pairs at
  remaining Phase-1 depth \(r_1 = 2\) in a single L1-cached byte lookup.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) Every generator of
\(H = \langle U, D, R^2, L^2, F^2, B^2 \rangle\) is invertible inside
\(H\); if \(x \cdot h \in H\) with \(h \in H\), then
\(x = (x \cdot h) h^{-1} \in H\), contradicting \(x \notin H\). The
remaining \(18 - 10 = 8\) generators of \(G\) are precisely
\(\{F, F' : F \in \mathcal E\}\). (2) An equatorial face
\(F \in \mathcal E\) contains \(2\) equatorial slice slots and \(2\) U/D
slots; a quarter-turn \(F^{\pm 1}\) swaps those \(2\) equatorial slots
with \(2\) U/D slots while fixing the other \(2\) equatorial slots.
Since \(F\) and \(F'\) move the \textbf{same} unordered 4-subset of edge
slots around a 4-cycle, \(F(s_\star) = F'(s_\star)\) as unordered
4-subsets, yielding exactly \(|\mathcal E| = 4\) distance-1 slice states
(each holding \(2\) slice edges in \(E\)-layer and \(2\) on face \(F\)).
From each of the \(4\) distance-1 states, applying the \(18\) outer
turns reaches \(55\) distinct distance-2 subsets, verified exhaustively
across all \(495 \times 18 = 8,910\) transitions (\texttt{702\ /\ 8,910}
surviving pairs, pruning \(8,208 / 8,910 = 92.19\%\)). ∎

\hypertarget{k2-quarter-turn-sister-fiber-involution-and-joint-coboundary}{%
\subsection{K2 quarter-turn sister-fiber involution and joint coboundary}\label{k2-quarter-turn-sister-fiber-involution-and-joint-coboundary}}

Proposition 8 implies that every pre-entry state \(x \in G \setminus H\)
at remaining depth \(r_1 = 1\) enters \(H\) via some equatorial face
\(F \in \mathcal E\). What happens to the opposite quarter-turn on that
same face?

\textbf{Proposition 9 --- \(K_2\) sister-fiber involution and joint
Phase-2 coboundary.} Let \(x \in G \setminus H\) be any state such that
\(y_+ = x \cdot F \in H\) for an equatorial quarter-turn
\(F \in \{R, F, L, B\}\). Define \(y_- = x \cdot F'\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(Identical quotient projection)} \(y_- \in H\), and \((F, F')\)
  induce \textbf{identical} transitions on all three Phase-1 coordinates
  (\texttt{co}, \texttt{eo}, \texttt{sl}). Thus \(y_+\) and \(y_-\)
  arise as twin lifts of the exact same Phase-1 prefix up to \(x\).
\item
  \emph{(\(K_2\) involution in \(H\))} Inside the subgroup \(H\),
  \(y_- = y_+ \cdot F^2\) and \(y_+ = y_- \cdot F^2\), where
  \(F^2 \in H\) is a single unit-cost Phase-2 generator. Consequently,
  \(|h_2(y_+) - h_2(y_-)| \le 1\), and
  \((D_{\mathcal A}(y_+), D_{\mathcal A}(y_-))\) is recovered from
  \textbf{one} steepest-descent root walk on \(y_+\) plus a single
  \(O(1)\) coboundary step along \(F^2\): \[
  \begin{aligned}
  D_{\mathcal A}(y_-) &= D_{\mathcal A}(y_+) - [j_{F^2} \in M_-(a_+)]\\
  &\quad + [j_{F^2} \notin M_0(a_+)].
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{13}
  \]
\item
  \emph{(Seam-admissible sister pruning)} Under non-redundant face
  concatenation (\texttt{face(q\_1)\ !=\ F}), the minimum total move
  length from \(y_+\) (or \(y_-\)) satisfies
  \(\ell(y_\pm) \ge d_1 + \tilde D_C(y_\pm, F)\), where
  \(\tilde D_C(y, F) = D_C(y) + [A_F^{(2)} \cap M_-(c, s_p) = \emptyset] + [A_F^{(2)} \cap M_0(c, s_p) = \emptyset, M_-(c, s_p) \ne \emptyset]\)
  for \(y \ne e\) (and \(0\) at \(y=e\)), allowing individual rejection
  without invalidating the sister lift when \(y_- = e, y_+ = F^2\).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) \(F' = F \cdot F^2\), so
\(y_- = x F' = (x F) F^2 = y_+ F^2\). Because \(y_+ \in H\) and
\(F^2 \in H\), group closure gives \(y_- \in H\), where
\((\text{co}, \text{eo}, \text{sl})\) takes the unique solved coset
value \((0, 0, s_\star)\) on both \(y_+\) and \(y_-\). (Even at
arbitrary \((c_1, e_1, s_1)\), \(F^2 \in H\) acts trivially on \(G/H\),
so \(\pi(xF) = \pi(xF')\) globally across all \(18,920\) coordinate-face
pairs.) (2) Since \((F^2)^2 = I\), right-multiplication by \(F^2\) is a
fixed-point-free involution pairing every Phase-2 coordinate state
\((c_+, q_+)\) and \((e_+, q_+)\) into a \(K_2\) edge of \(H\);
Proposition 5(2) yields Eq. (13) in \(O(1)\), verified exhaustively
across all \(1,935,360 \times 4 = 7,741,440\) sister transitions. (3) If
\(y_\pm \ne e\), any Phase-2 completion after terminal Phase-1 face
\(F\) must begin with some \(j \in A_F^{(2)}\); if no
\(j \in A_F^{(2)}\) decreases \(D_C\), Phase 2 requires at least
\(D_C + 1\) moves (and \(D_C + 2\) if every \(j \in A_F^{(2)}\)
increases \(D_C\)). Applying the test separately to \(y_+\) and \(y_-\)
preserves soundness when \(y_- = e\) (\(0\) moves) and \(y_+ = F^2\)
(\(\tilde D_C(F^2, F) \ge 3\) under forbidden same-face \(F^2\)). ∎

\hypertarget{phase-crossing-bellman-potentials-and-inline-zero-slack-sieve}{%
\subsection{Phase-crossing Bellman potentials and inline zero-slack sieve}\label{phase-crossing-bellman-potentials-and-inline-zero-slack-sieve}}

Can Phase 1 foresee high subgroup holonomy \(h_2(y)\) \textbf{before}
crossing into \(H\)? Although Phase-2 slice permutation
\(q \in \operatorname{Sym}_4\) is undefined outside \(H\) (equatorial
edges leave the middle layer), ambient outer turns act globally on
corner permutation \(c \in \operatorname{Sym}_8\) (\(40,320\) states)
and on the ordered 4-tuples of U-edges, D-edges, and E-slice edges in
\(P(12, 4) = 12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9 = 11,880\).

\textbf{Proposition 10 --- Admissible phase-crossing Bellman potentials
and zero-slack holonomy sieve.}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  For corners \(c \in \operatorname{Sym}_8\), define
  \(\Psi_0^C(c) = \min_{q \in \operatorname{Sym}_4} D_C(c, q)\), and for
  \(r \in \{1, 2\}\) define recursively over equatorial quarter-turns
  \(E_1 = \{F, F' : F \in \mathcal E\}\) and all 18 turns \(G_1\): \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \Psi_1^C(c) &= \min_{m \in E_1} \Psi_0^C(\delta_C(c, m)),\\
  \Psi_2^C(c) &= \min_{m \in G_1} \Psi_1^C(\delta_C(c, m)).
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{14}
  \] Define \(\Psi_r^{\rm EU}, \Psi_r^{\rm ED}, \Psi_r^{\rm ES}\) on
  \(P(12,4)\) analogously from exact \(H\)-distances of U-edges,
  D-edges, and E-slice edges (\(\infty\) outside \(H\) at \(r=0\)). Then
  for \textbf{every} ambient state \(x \in G \setminus H\) and
  \textbf{every} completion \(w = m_1 \dots m_r\) (\(r \in \{1,2\}\))
  with \(x \cdot w = y \in H\): \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \Psi_r(x) := \max\bigl\{&\Psi_r^C(x), \Psi_r^{\rm EU}(x),\\
  &\Psi_r^{\rm ED}(x), \Psi_r^{\rm ES}(x)\bigr\} \le h_2(y).
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{15}
  \] Hence any branch at Phase-1 depth \(g_1\) with remaining depth
  \(r \in \{1,2\}\) satisfying \(g_1 + r + \Psi_r(x) \ge B\) cannot
  improve incumbent bound \(B\) and is pruned \textbf{with zero loss of
  bounded-shell optimality}.
\item
  \emph{(Inline zero-slack holonomy sieve)} Whenever a harvested fiber
  \(y \in H\) at depth \(d_1\) satisfies \(b_0 = d_1 + h_2(y) < B\),
  running Phase-2 coboundary Horizon-2 verification strictly at
  \(\sigma_2 = 0\) (depth \(r_2 = h_2(y)\), branching restricted purely
  to \(M_-^{(2)}\)) immediately lowers \(B \leftarrow b_0\) whenever
  \(y\) is slack-free inside \(H\).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) By Proposition 8(1), any length-\(r\) completion \(w\)
from \(x \notin H\) into \(H\) has \(m_r \in E_1\). At
\(y = x \cdot w \in H\) with slice coordinate \(q_y\),
\(\Psi_0^C(c_y) = \min_q D_C(c_y, q) \le D_C(c_y, q_y) \le h_2(y)\),
while \(D_E(e_y, q_y)\) dominates the separate U-edge, D-edge, and
E-slice distances inside \(H\) because \((e_y, q_y)\) fixes all 12 edge
positions in \(H\). Backward induction over \(m_r \in E_1\) and
\(m_1 \in G_1\) yields
\(\Psi_r^{\mathcal A}(x) \le \Psi_0^{\mathcal A}(y) \le h_2(y)\) for
each component, so their maximum satisfies Eq. (15). Clamping values to
\(\min(15, \cdot)\) packs each table into 4-bit nibbles (\(1,367,280\)
entries = \(683,640\) B total, verified across all transitions). (2) At
\(\sigma_2 = 0\), every move in a valid completion of length \(h_2(y)\)
must decrease every coordinate attaining \(h_2(y)\); if such a path
exists, \(b_0 = d_1 + h_2(y) < B\) is a verified solution in \(G\),
tightening \(B\) for all subsequent Phase-1 branches. ∎

\hypertarget{defect-stratified-eikonal-wavefront-and-bounded-shell-optimality}{%
\subsection{Defect-stratified Eikonal wavefront and bounded-shell optimality}\label{defect-stratified-eikonal-wavefront-and-bounded-shell-optimality}}

Combining Propositions 8--10 yields \textbf{Algorithm 3 (HBEW)}. Rather
than running deep \(\sigma_2 > 0\) Phase-2 searches as soon as an
arbitrary fiber \(y\) is encountered, HBEW places surviving fibers into
two-dimensional buckets \(Q[b_0][d_1]\) indexed by zero-slack arrival
bound \(b_0 = d_1 + h_2(y)\), then sweeps candidate total lengths
\(b = 1, 2, \dots\) in strictly monotone waves
(\texttt{sigma2\ =\ b\ -\ b0} across all \(b_0 \le b\)).

\begin{figure}[t]
\hrule\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Algorithm 3.} Holonomy-Guided Bifurcated Eikonal Wavefront Search (HBEW)
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\footnotesize,breaklines=true,breakanywhere=true]
Input: holonomy-compiled tables (T, H), legal state s, cap D1, window delta, target tau
if s in H: return exact Phase-2 coboundary IDA* from s (split = 0)
B = 31; Q[0..31][0..12] = empty; compute root h1(s) via 1-form walks
for shell d1 = h1(s) to min(D1, h1(s) + delta):
  if d1 >= B or B <= tau: break
  DFS Phase 1 with 24-bit SWAR masks & hoisted child pruning:
    at r1 = 2: mask moves by slDist2Mask[sl]; check g1 + 2 + Psi2(x) < B
    at r1 = 1: restrict to positive equatorial turns F in {R,F,L,B}
      check g1 + 1 + Psi1(x) < B; lift K2 sister pair (y+, y-) in H
      recover (dc+, de+) once; compute (dc-, de-) in O(1) via Eq. (13)
      for each sister y in {y+, y-} passing seam bound < B:
        b0 = d1 + h2(y)
        if zero-slack Horizon-2 probe(y, h2(y)) succeeds:
          B = b0; record best path; if B <= tau: stop shell
        else: push y into bucket Q[b0][d1]
if B > tau:
  for candidate total length b = h1(s) to B - 1:
    for b0 = 0 to b, and d1 = 1 to 12:
      for each fiber y in Q[b0][d1]: // exact stratum sigma2 = b - b0 >= 1
        if Phase-2 coboundary H2 search(y, b - d1) succeeds:
          B = b; record best path; if B <= tau: return best path
          goto next_done
next_done: return verified best path
\end{Verbatim}
\vspace{3pt}\hrule
\end{figure}

\textbf{Theorem 1 --- Soundness and exact bounded-shell optimality of
HBEW.} Let
\(\mathcal T_H^{\le \bar D}(s) = \{(p, q) : |p| \le \bar D,\ s \cdot p \in H,\ (s \cdot p) \cdot q = e,\ \text{face}(p_{\text{last}}) \ne \text{face}(q_1)\}\)
be the space of all non-redundant two-phase solutions within effective
shell cap \(\bar D = \min(D_1, h_1(s)+\delta)\).

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{(Soundness)} Every trajectory returned by Algorithm 3 is a
  verified legal factorization of \(s^{-1}\) in \(G\) with zero adjacent
  same-face turns.
\item
  \emph{(Exact bounded-shell optimality at \(\tau = 0\))} When
  \(\tau = 0\) and no node/time cap intervenes, Algorithm 3 returns a
  trajectory attaining the \textbf{exact global minimum}
  \(\min_{(p,q) \in \mathcal T_H^{\le \bar D}(s)} (|p| + |q|)\),
  invariant to toggling any subset of its four pruning pillars (UD-slice
  funnel, \(K_2\) sister pairing, \(\Psi\)-potentials, and zero-slack /
  \(\sigma_2\) stratification).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (1) Phase-2 entry enforces
\texttt{seamAllowed2{[}lastFace{]}} (\texttt{face(q\_1)\ !=\ lastFace}),
canonical Phase-1/Phase-2 masks forbid consecutive same-face moves
inside each phase, and cubie + 54-sticker replay verifies
\((s \cdot p) \cdot q = e\). (2) Let
\((p^\star, q^\star) \in \mathcal T_H^{\le \bar D}(s)\) achieve minimal
total length \(L^\star = d_1^\star + d_2^\star\) with
\(y^\star = s \cdot p^\star \in H\). If \(d_1^\star = 0\) (\(s \in H\)),
Phase-2 IDA* from \(s\) increases \(r_2 = h_2(s), h_2(s)+1, \dots\) and
finds \(L^\star\) directly. If \(d_1^\star \ge 1\), assume without loss
of generality that \(p^\star\) first enters \(H\) at step \(d_1^\star\)
(otherwise split at its first \(H\)-hit, preserving \(L^\star\) and
reducing \(|p^\star| \le \bar D\)). By Proposition 8, \(p^\star\) passes
through a valid distance-2 slice at \(r_1=2\) and ends with \(F\) or
\(F'\) (\(F \in \mathcal E\)) at \(r_1=1\); Proposition 9 visits both
\(y_+ = x F\) and \(y_- = x F'\) while preserving seam admissibility;
and Proposition 10 ensures \(\Psi_r(x) \le h_2(y^\star) \le d_2^\star\),
so \(g_1 + r + \Psi_r(x) \le d_1^\star + d_2^\star = L^\star\). Because
no valid solution can set \(B < L^\star\), \(p^\star\) is never pruned
(\(L^\star < B\) holds strictly until a length-\(L^\star\) solution is
recorded). If \(d_2^\star = h_2(y^\star)\) (\(\sigma_2 = 0\)), the
inline zero-slack probe finds a length-\(L^\star\) completion
immediately (\texttt{B\ =\ L*}); if \(d_2^\star > h_2(y^\star)\)
(\(\sigma_2 \ge 1\)), \(y^\star\) enters
\(Q[d_1^\star + h_2(y^\star)][d_1^\star]\), and the outer wavefront loop
tests \(b = h_1(s), \dots, L^\star\) in strictly increasing order,
finding a solution of length \(L^\star\) at or before
\((b = L^\star, y^\star)\) and terminating immediately when
\(\tau = 0\). ∎

All runs execute single-threaded on Node 18.19.1 (Linux x64, AMD EPYC

solver-order rotation and alternating forward/reverse corpus traversal.

(\textasciitilde{}\(48\) MiB) exceeds a single 32 MiB L3 slice and
artificially inflates cache misses, the Stage-2 benchmark runs: (1) a

Every single solve is verified for identical move sequences, splits,
logical visit counts, and external 54-sticker replay outside the timed
interval. Paired 95\% confidence intervals use 2,000 seeded
paired-instance bootstrap resamples.

\hypertarget{limitations}{%
\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}}

\textbf{Bounded-shell versus unrestricted God's-number optimality.}
Theorem 1 proves exact optimality over \(\mathcal T_H^{\le \bar D}(s)\),
the space of two-phase factorizations entering \(H\) within
\(\bar D \le D_1\) moves. A globally shortest \(\le 20\)-move path in
\(G\) whose prefixes up to length \(\bar D\) never enter \(H\) lies
outside \(\mathcal T_H^{\le \bar D}(s)\) (which is why \(0.2\%\) of
\texttt{hbewExact} states at \(D_1=11\) take \(23\) moves while
\(99.8\%\) take \(\le 22\)). Nor do we claim certified worldwide
priority or any change in exponential worst-case search complexity.

\textbf{Storage and compilation tradeoffs.} Full dual-horizon
compilation (\texttt{11.49} MiB) and HBEW holonomy compilation
(\texttt{14.03} MiB total, \(+2.54\) MiB, \(+219.1\) ms compile time)
expand persistent typed-array storage relative to raw scalar tables
(\texttt{5.55} MiB). Peak V8 heap and physical mobile-device cache
behavior remain unmeasured.

\textbf{Search policy and comparator scope.} COVE-3 count-budget
equivalence holds whenever wall-clock deadlines do not intervene. HBEW
intentionally operates on a single coordinate axis without inverse-cube
probing, pre-moves, or \(D_{4h}\) symmetry compression
(\texttt{min2phase} style) so its mathematical bundle mechanisms can be
isolated cleanly; combining HBEW with 3-axis symmetry conjugation is
left to future work. Only 3×3 two-phase/holonomy solving is compiled.

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

Across three cumulative stages, we showed how discrete differential and
bundle geometry transform both the \textbf{compiler} and the
\textbf{search algorithm} of two-phase Rubik's Cube solving.
Representing heuristic tables purely by their discrete
coboundaries---24-bit Cayley-\(K_4\) clique syndromes in Phase 1 and a
unified \(24,743\)-entry potential-free Horizon-2 dictionary with
three-tier ghost accounting in Phase 2---cuts generated visits by
\(93.62\%\) down to \(1.43\)--\(1.84\) ms at 100\% identical scalar
trajectories. Lifting the search itself onto the principal \(H\)-bundle
\(G \to G/H\), \textbf{Holonomy-Guided Bifurcated Eikonal Wavefront
Search (HBEW)} funnels trajectories through the \(4/495\) UD-slice
bottleneck, co-integrates \(K_2\) quarter-turn sister fibers
\(y_- = y_+ F^2\), forecasts subgroup holonomy above \(\partial H\) via
4-bit Bellman potentials, and verifies surviving fibers in monotone
\(\sigma_2\) strata---achieving \(20.58\) mean moves (\(100\% \le 22\))
on 500 held-out uniform states and strictly Pareto-dominating
\texttt{cubejs\ 1.3.2} in both speed (\(6.54\times\) median,
\(15.7\times\) P95) and solution length (\(20.46\) vs.~\(21.71\) moves).
